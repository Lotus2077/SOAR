## Decision rule

**How the grades are read,** stated before the runs.

- **Decided (owner, 2026-11-17):** By what test is a difference between the arms called
  shown? *Accepted as proposed:* the reading of E0's Amendment 4, item 5, as the bullets
  below state it. Reason: it is the reading the E0 card pre-registered, and no source sets
  a pass level for E2. (Item 11.)
  - **No pass level is set.** Each answer is given as the two arms' counts of accepts,
    of 4.
  - **Graded runs only.** The 8 runs on the two synthetic tasks are counted. r3's 4 are
    not.
  - **A difference is called shown** only where Fisher's exact test on the two counts
    gives a two-sided p below 0.05.
  - **With 4 graded runs an arm, that is one case:** 4 accepts against 0, p = 0.029. A
    count of 4 against 1, or 3 against 0, gives p = 0.14 and is not shown.
  - **The test pools the two tasks.** It treats an arm's four runs as independent draws.
    They are two runs on each of two packets. An exact test stratified by task gives 4
    against 0 a two-sided p of 2/36 = 0.056. So by that stricter test no count at this
    sample is shown.
    - **The pooled test is the card's test** (decision 10 of BL-20261117-0941-group-a-decided-under-delegation).
      The session chose it under the owner's direction of 2026-11-17, because the
      accepted rule's own counts are the pooled ones. The result entry reports the
      stratified p beside it.
  - **Anything else is "no difference shown",** whichever arm the counts favour. That is
    not a finding that the writers are alike.
  - **A tie** is equal counts. **A near-tie** is counts one apart: one changed grade
    would erase or reverse it. Both are read as "no difference shown".
  - **The rule and the test can differ.** Case 3 below can pick writer-2 on counts the
    test calls "no difference shown", such as 2 against 0 (p = 0.43). The result entry
    then states both and calls the pick an investment choice.
  - **Critical-check counts, calls, tokens and times** are reported for each run as
    recorded. No test is applied to them, and they decide nothing by themselves.

**The rule.** It picks a writer for E3 and E4. Like E3's rule, it is an investment rule
and not a significance claim (review §5.6, "Decision rules").

- **Decided (owner, 2026-11-17):** What result changes the E3 and E4 writer?
  *Accepted as proposed:* Heavy stays unless writer-2 earns the change, by the four cases
  below, taken in order. Reason: the review keeps Heavy until E2. Its frontier rule,
  itself still proposed, adopts a change only on a gain beyond tolerance (review §5.2,
  stage S8; §2.1). (Item 12.)
  - **Case 1, the bound stopped a graded run.** If any of writer-2's 4 graded runs
    ended at its bound, Heavy stays. Another bound is a new hypothesis and needs an
    amendment or a new card.
  - **What "ended at its bound" means.** The run's row shows all three of these:
    - a `runStatus` other than `submitted`;
    - a `runClass` cause of `task`;
    - a `terminalCause` of `bounded_allowance_exhausted`, `cancelled_or_deadline`,
      `deadline_before_request`, `deadline_before_completion` or `session_deadline`
      (runner.ts; src/main/private-agent/model.ts; session.ts).
  - **Case 2, few accepts in both arms.** If both arms have 0 or 1 accepts, the round is
    not read for the bound: the packet, the tasks or the write prompt limited both.
    Heavy stays by default, and the result entry names one next lever.
  - **Case 3, two or more accepts apart.** If writer-2 leads by two or more, writer-2 is
    picked once the owner records it. If writer-1 leads by two or more, Heavy stays.
  - **Case 4, a tie or a near-tie.** The rule picks nobody. The result entry puts the
    counts, the checks, the times and the bound's use to the owner. Heavy stays until
    the owner records a choice.
  - **What the rule cannot do.** It never picks writer-2 on equal counts, the outcome
    the hypothesis predicts. The owner then weighs the calls and time the smaller bound
    saved against grades that show no difference.
  - **Provisional grades.** Where the owner grades, the rule is applied to the owner's
    verdicts.

**What each measurement can and cannot show at this sample.**

- **Accept counts, 4 against 4.** By the pooled test they can show a difference only at
  4 against 0. By a test stratified by task they can show none. They cannot show that
  the writers are alike, and a real gap of one or two accepts in four stays invisible.
- **Bound stops, 0 of 4.** Four graded runs that all submit do not show the bound is
  safe. That count is still consistent, at a one-sided 95% level, with a true stop rate
  of about half (1 − 0.05^(1/4) = 0.53). That figure also treats the four runs as
  independent, which two runs a packet are not.
- **Two tasks.** Both are synthetic and, under item 5, smaller than f1 to f3. The result
  shows nothing about E3's packets or about web-scale work.
- **Two repeats.** They show whether a task's two runs agree. They are too few to
  estimate a variance.
- **r3's four runs** show whether the procedure works end to end and how each writer
  behaves on an exposed task. Under item 6 they count for no arm.
- **Packet attempts** are supply for the writers. Their count is recorded and is not an
  estimate of a completion rate.
