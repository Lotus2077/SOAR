## Hypothesis

On the owner's own real jobs, the desktop build's existing local routes return results the
owner accepts, at zero fee. The test is the review's: at least 3 of 5 jobs meet the OAJ
conditions of PLAN.md section 1 (review row E5).

Two things are tested at once:
- **Need.** The owner hands real work to the build. The record so far is one owner-run
  job, on 2026-10-12, and none since (review section 1, "Need").
- **Acceptance.** The owner accepts what comes back, under the OAJ conditions.

E5 makes no claim that a deliverable is correct. It makes none about research, a cloud
route or private data.

**The decision E5 changes** (review row E5, last column):

- **0:** no route work until the cause is named.

**When a job is an OAJ** (PLAN.md section 1). All six conditions must hold. Beside each
is what a row can show of it:
1. **The owner chose it, and it is real work.** Not on the row. It rests on the owner's
   statement (O7).
2. **It ran in the normal app build, not a test driver.** Not shown by the row. The
   writer checks a receipt's keys and value shapes, not where it came from. Any build
   with #40 exports the same receipt. The receipt carries no build commit, so the row's
   lesson names the tag, on the owner's statement.
3. **It was exported through the app.** `exportedThroughApp` is the owner's declaration.
   The app records no export (BL-20261108-0905, Limitations).
4. **Accept within 72 hours, or Accept with at most 10 minutes of edits.**
   - `outcome` is `accepted`, and `verdictAt` gives the time of the verdict.
   - The row's `date` is the UTC date the task started. So the 72 hours can be checked to
     the day only.
   - `ownerMinutes` holds review and edit time together
     (src/shared/general-task-contracts.ts). At 10 or less the edit limit is met. Above 10
     the row cannot tell.
5. **At most one other manual intervention.** `interventions` counts them, and an edit
   pass counts as one (README). The row does not say which kind each was. Up to 1 always
   passes. 2 passes only if one was the edit pass.
6. **The heavy floor: 30 minutes saved by the owner's estimate, or 15 agent actions.**
   `toolCalls` of 15 or more passes; the job of 2026-10-12 was read that way
   (BL-20261012-1610-first-owner-accepted-job). Otherwise it rests on the owner's
   estimate, which no record holds (O7).

So `accepted` on a row is not by itself an OAJ. The closing entry counts a job only when
all six hold, and says which rest on the owner's statement.

- **O4, what "3 of 5" counts.** **Decided (owner, 2026-11-17):** which five jobs the rule
  reads, and whether they are pooled across the routes. *Accepted as proposed:* option A
  below, because OAJ is the adoption measure and a job that never finishes is a failed
  hand-off.
  - **A, the first five started.** The first five jobs the owner starts from day 1, on
    the three routes, pooled. A job that ends without submitting counts as not accepted. A
    job cancelled before its first model call is no job: on 2026-10-12 a task cancelled at
    creation was not counted (BL-20261012-1610-first-owner-accepted-job).
    - **Shows:** whether handing real work to the build ends in an accepted result,
      failures to finish included.
    - **Source it follows:** the review's criterion 8, which counts a harness run that
      does not submit as rejected (review section 2.2).
  - **B, the first five submitted.** The first five jobs that reach `submitted` and get a
    verdict, pooled.
    - **Shows:** acceptance of delivered work only. A build that often fails to finish
      could still pass.
    - **Source it follows:** PLAN.md Phase 4's "OAJ ≥ 4 of ≥ 5 submitted". Decision 8 of
      BL-20261108-0904-owner-verdict-capture-design also reads OAJ as counted over
      submitted jobs. PLAN's "submitted" may instead mean jobs the owner handed in.
  - **C, five on each route.** Each route is read on its own first five.
    - **Shows:** a result per route, which "marks the routes `Verified`" suggests.
    - **Cost:** 15 jobs. At the 2 owner hours a week that PLAN.md asks for (D9), 14 days
      would probably not reach them.
  - **D, five in a week.** At least 3 accepted of at least 5 in one calendar week.
    - **Shows:** a weekly rate, which is OAJ's own unit (PLAN.md section 1).
    - **Cost:** five jobs in one week, and a rule for which week is read.
  - **Which routes a pass marks** is O13.
  - **Re-runs.** A second run of the same work is `exposed` and is not a new job among
    the five (PLAN.md section 7).
  - **A job that ended on infrastructure** counts as not accepted, unless a re-run flagged
    under the card's rule replaces it (README, `runClass`). The rule, accepted with O4, is
    one re-run, flagged in its row's lesson, as the review allows one zero-fee resume
    (section 5.1, rule 5).

**How the counts are read,** stated before any job:
- **The pass level has a source.** It is the row's 3 of 5. No other level is set.
- **It is an investment rule, not a rate.** PLAN.md section 6 answers small samples with
  investment-style rules, and calls real jobs a sequential replication.
  - By the exact binomial interval at 95%, 3 of 5 fits a true acceptance chance anywhere
    from about 15% to 95%.
  - 0 of 5 still fits a chance of up to about 52%. 5 of 5 fits one as low as about 48%.
  - If each job had a 40% chance, 3 or more of 5 would still come about one time in three.
    At a 60% chance, 2 or fewer would come about one time in three.
  - The jobs are not independent draws. The owner picks them, and they differ in family
    and in difficulty.
- **One rater, not blind.** The owner knows the route and is the only judge. E5 has no
  planted defect and no arm guess, which PLAN.md section 6 lists against rubber-stamp
  verdicts.
- **A verdict is the OAJ mark, not a correctness grade.** The task's independent
  acceptance stays `not_evaluated` (README, "Owner verdict rows").
- **No arms are compared.** Counts by route and by week are reported as counts.
- **O11, differences between routes or weeks.** **Decided (owner, 2026-11-17):** by what
  test a difference between two routes, or two weeks, is called shown.
  *Accepted as proposed:* the rule of E0's Amendment 4, item 5, so that one rule serves
  both cards.
  - A difference is called shown only where Fisher's exact test on the two counts gives a
    two-sided p below 0.05.
  - Anything else is "no difference shown". That is not a finding that two routes are
    alike.
  - With five jobs split between two routes, no outcome reaches that level: the smallest
    p is 0.10. With five jobs a side, only 5 against 0, 5 against 1 or 4 against 0 does.
- **O13, what a pass marks.** **Decided (owner, 2026-11-17):** which routes a pass marks
  `Verified`, and what the mark covers. *Accepted as proposed:* a route is marked only if
  it holds at least one accepted job among the five, because the rows can show a count and
  no more.
  - **What the mark covers,** as decided: one thing. The counted rows exist as written and
    give the count stated, after the model of BL-20261116-1357-e0-verified.
  - **What it does not cover:** correctness, a rate, or a route with no accepted job.
  - **Where the question comes from.** The row says only that a pass "marks the routes
    `Verified`". No source says which routes, or what the mark covers.
  - **The alternative** marks no route singly. The closing entry records only that the
    pooled count passed, because a route could otherwise be marked on one job.
